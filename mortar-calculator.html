Short answer

1,000 standard UK bricks in a half-brick wall need about 0.3 m³ of wet mortar: roughly five 25 kg bags of cement and half a tonne of building sand at a 1 : 4 mix, including 10% for waste. A solid wall one brick thick uses about 30% more mortar per brick, because of the vertical joint down its middle. In the US, 1,000 modular bricks in a single wythe take about 8 cu ft of mortar before waste.

Which mortar mix should I use?

Mortar should be a little weaker than the bricks it joins, so any cracking follows the joints, which are easy to repoint, rather than the bricks. Common rules of thumb:

  • 1 : 3 cement : sand for copings, work below the damp-proof course, retaining walls and dense engineering bricks.
  • 1 : 4 with a plasticiser for most general outdoor walls in ordinary facing bricks.
  • 1 : 5 or 1 : 6 with a plasticiser for softer bricks and sheltered or internal walls.
  • Lime mortar for old, soft brickwork; hard cement mortar can trap moisture and spall old bricks (see repointing tips).
  • US mortar types: Type N for most above-grade walls, Type S for below-grade, retaining and high-wind work, Type M for heavy loads below grade.

Follow a structural engineer's or the brick maker's specification where there is one.

How the calculation works

Each brick, with its share of joints, occupies (brick length + joint) × (brick height + joint) of wall face. Mortar is the wall's volume minus the bricks' volume. A wall one brick thick also has a vertical collar joint between its two leaves. Wet mortar is converted to dry materials using the common estimating rule that dry ingredients shrink by about 30% when mixed, with loose bulk densities of about 1,440 kg/m³ for cement and 1,600 kg/m³ for damp building sand. These are the same formulas as the brick calculator.

Mortar FAQ

How much mortar do I need per 1,000 bricks?

For standard UK bricks (215 × 102.5 × 65 mm) with a 10 mm joint, about 0.30 m³ of wet mortar per 1,000 bricks in a half-brick (single-leaf) wall, or about 0.38 m³ in a solid wall one brick thick, before waste. US modular brick with a ⅜ in joint takes about 8 cu ft per 1,000 in a single wythe. Frogged bricks laid frog-up take more.

How many bags of cement do I need for 1,000 bricks?

About 111 kg of cement for 1,000 UK bricks in a half-brick wall at a 1:4 cement-to-sand mix, which is five 25 kg bags once you add 10% for waste (about 122 kg). A one-brick-thick wall needs about 143 kg before waste, so six bags.

How much sand do I need for 1,000 bricks?

About 0.5 tonnes (495 kg) of building sand for 1,000 UK bricks in a half-brick wall at a 1:4 mix, or about 545 kg with 10% waste. A one-brick-thick wall needs about 635 kg before waste.

How much mortar do I need per square metre of brickwork?

About 18 litres (0.018 m³) of wet mortar per m² for a UK half-brick wall with 10 mm joints, and about 45 litres per m² for a solid wall one brick thick, because the thick wall also has a vertical collar joint down the middle.

What mortar mix should I use for brickwork?

For general outdoor brickwork 1 part cement to 4 parts building sand (with plasticiser) is the common choice. Use a stronger 1:3 mix for copings, work below the damp-proof course and engineering bricks, and a weaker 1:5 or 1:6 for soft bricks and sheltered or internal walls. Mortar should be weaker than the brick, and old soft brickwork usually needs a lime mortar. In the US, Type N is the usual mortar for above-grade walls and Type S for below-grade and retaining walls.
