Hot weather bricklaying: keeping mortar workable in heat and wind

Short answer

Hot weather rules apply above 100 °F (37.8 °C), or above 90 °F (32.2 °C) with wind over 8 mph. Then mortar must stay below 120 °F (48.9 °C), be used within 2 hours of initial mixing instead of the usual 2½, be retempered with cool water, and new masonry should be fog sprayed at least three times a day for three days.

The two hot weather bands

Air temperatureRequirements
Above 100 °F, or above 90 °F with wind over 8 mphKeep sand piles damp and loose. Keep mortar below 120 °F. Flush the mixer, mortar pans and boards with cool water before they touch mortar. Retempering with cool water to hold consistency. Use mortar within 2 hours of initial mixing. Fog spray new masonry until damp at least three times a day until it is three days old (based on mean daily temperature).
Above 115 °F, or above 105 °F with wind over 8 mphAll of the above, plus shade materials and mixing equipment from direct sun and use cool mixing water. Ice is permitted in the mixing water but must be melted or removed before the water meets the other ingredients.

Source: TMS Specification requirements as summarised in BIA Technical Note 1. Between 40 °F and 100 °F normal procedures apply.

What heat does to mortar

The main problem is water leaving the mortar too fast, by evaporation and by suction into dry brick. Hot mortar loses plasticity quickly. If it stiffens before the cement has hydrated enough, it can flash set, and the bond to the brick suffers. That is why the guidance favours retempering with cool water instead of fighting a stiff mix.

BIA recommends mortar ingredients that hold water, such as lime and evenly graded sand, for hot weather work. That is the opposite of the cold weather advice in our cold weather guide.

Practical habits on a hot day

  • Mix smaller batches so each one is used well inside the 2 hour window.
  • Spray sand piles and leave them uncovered. Evaporation cools them, and damp sand heats more slowly.
  • Watch very absorbent brick. BIA's preferred method for high suction brick is wetting it 3 to 24 hours before use, so the surface is damp but not wet when laid.
  • Use windscreens where drying wind is the problem, and cover the top of the wall at the end of the day with a membrane running at least 2 ft down each side.
  • Do not overload the board. Wooden boards and sun baked pans draw water out of mortar. Shade and flush them.

Hot weather bricklaying FAQ

Is it too hot to lay bricks?

Bricklaying can continue in heat, but above 100 °F (or 90 °F with wind over 8 mph) hot weather procedures apply: mortar kept below 120 °F, used within 2 hours, retempered with cool water, and new work fog sprayed for three days.

Should I wet bricks before laying in hot weather?

Highly absorbent brick benefits from wetting, ideally 3 to 24 hours before use so the surface has dried back. Brick with low absorption should not be soaked.

Can I add ice to mortar?

Ice can cool the mixing water, but it must be fully melted or removed before the water is combined with the cement, lime and sand.

How long do I fog spray new brickwork?

Under the hot weather provisions, until damp at least three times a day until the masonry is three days old.
