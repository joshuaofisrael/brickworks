How Far Apart Should Brick Wall Ties Be?

Spacing, embedment, tie types and corrosion for bricklayers, with sources

Short answer

Space wall ties exactly as the drawings or your code require. For anchored brick veneer over wood studs, BIA Technical Note 28 summarizes the prescriptive limits as at least one tie for every 2.67 sq ft of wall, no more than 32 in. apart horizontally and 24 in. apart vertically under the IRC (25 in. under the IBC), plus extra ties within 12 in. of openings larger than 16 in. High wind and seismic areas need more ties, and masonry backed cavity walls follow different tables. Wall ties carry wind load from the brick to the backing, so they are a structural item: this page explains the published rules, it is not engineering advice, and your local code and drawings win.

1. What a wall tie does

CMHA TEK 12-01B separates masonry connectors into wall ties, which connect one masonry wythe to another, and anchors, which connect masonry to a structural support or frame. BIA Technical Note 44B uses "wall tie" for both and notes that the ones fixing veneer to a backing are more properly called veneer anchors. In a veneer wall the backing carries the load and the brick is the finish, so the ties are what hold the brick leaf against wind. CMHA says connectors should be stiff out of the plane of the wall but still let the two wythes move differently in plane, which matters when clay brick is tied to concrete block, steel or timber.

CMHA also notes that codes require the type, size and location of connectors to be shown on the project drawings. That is the first place to look.

2. Spacing for brick veneer over wood studs

These are the prescriptive anchor rules summarized in BIA Technical Note 28 (2012 edition). Check them against the code edition your jurisdiction has adopted.

RuleBIA Technical Note 28 summary
Wall area per anchorAt least one anchor for each 2.67 sq ft of wall area
Maximum spacing32 in. horizontally; 24 in. vertically under the IRC, 25 in. under the IBC
OpeningsAdditional anchors within 12 in. of openings larger than 16 in., at a maximum spacing of 3 ft on center
High wind, IRCOne anchor for each 2 sq ft of wall area
High wind, IBCReduce wall area per anchor by 30 percent; no more than 18 in. horizontally and vertically
SeismicSDC D and above, and townhouses in SDC C: reduce wall area per anchor by 25 percent
FixingFix anchors to the studs through the sheathing, not to the sheathing alone, with a corrosion resistant 8d common nail or equivalent (8d ring shank in SDC D)
Air spaceAt least 1 in.; no more than 4 1/2 in. from the back of the brick to the sheathing unless the anchors are rationally designed

BIA Technical Note 44B adds a general recommendation to place additional ties within 8 in. of openings and discontinuities such as windows, shelf angles and vertical expansion joints. For concrete masonry backing and noncomposite cavity walls, CMHA points to its own spacing tables (TEK 03-06C for veneers and TEK 16-01A for composite and noncomposite walls) rather than the wood stud numbers above.

3. Which tie for which wall

  • Corrugated ties: Technical Note 44B says they are typically used in low rise residential veneer over wood frame and are not recommended over steel studs, in masonry backed cavity walls, multi wythe walls or grouted walls. Technical Note 28 calls for at least No. 22 U.S. gage, a maximum 1 in. air space where they are used, and the nail within 1/2 in. of the 90 degree bend.
  • Rectangular (box) and Z ties: used to bond two or more masonry wythes. Z ties should only be used with solid units (not less than 75 percent solid) or grouted units; rectangular ties work with solid or hollow units (Technical Note 44B).
  • Joint reinforcement: ladder or truss wire laid in the bed joints. Technical Note 44B says truss type joint reinforcement is not recommended in cavity walls or brick veneer with masonry backing because it can restrain movement between wythes, and CMHA recommends ladder type where significant differential movement is expected.
  • Adjustable two piece ties: useful when bed joints in the two leaves do not line up. Technical Note 44B says the code limits mechanical play to 1/16 in. and recommends reducing adjustment eccentricity by installing ties as the facing wythe is built.

Whatever the type, Technical Note 44B recommends ties without formed drips: testing showed a drip can reduce the ultimate buckling load by approximately 50 percent.

4. Embedment and the cavity

CMHA TEK 12-01B says the masonry specification requires connectors to be embedded at least 1 1/2 in. (38 mm) into a mortar bed of solid units, and unit ties in hollow masonry must extend completely across the hollow units. It also notes that current codes allow cavities up to 4 1/2 in. (114 mm) wide without a wall tie analysis; wider cavities for thicker insulation need engineered ties.

Keep the air space clear as the wall rises. Technical Note 28 says it is essential to maintain the air space between the veneer and the backing so water can drain down the back of the brick to the flashing and weeps, and that drainage products should not replace good workmanship and an effort to keep the cavity clean of mortar droppings. See weep holes in brick walls.

5. Corrosion protection

CMHA TEK 12-01B lists the coatings the masonry specification calls for. Carbon steel ties need galvanizing or epoxy; for wire ties and anchors in exterior walls that means hot dip galvanizing to ASTM A153 at 1.5 oz of zinc per sq ft. Where stainless steel is specified it must be AISI Type 304 or 316. Use the grade on the specification and do not swap in a lower one because it is what the merchant has.

6. Existing walls and remedial ties

Technical Note 44B describes re-anchoring systems (mechanical expansion, helical screw and epoxy adhesive types) used to add ties that were never installed, replace failed ties or attach new veneer to an old facade. It says many designers and contractors are not fully familiar with them, so consulting the tie system manufacturer is essential. A wall that is bulging, cracking or moving needs a proper assessment before anyone adds ties. BIA Technical Note 46, listed in the BIA Technical Notes index, covers wall tie replacement as part of maintenance.

Outside the US, the IRC, IBC and TMS rules above do not apply. Follow your national code and the tie maker's data for the exact product.

Wall tie FAQ

How far apart should wall ties be in brick veneer?

Follow the spacing on the drawings or the code your job is built to. For anchored brick veneer over wood studs, BIA Technical Note 28 summarizes the prescriptive limits as at least one anchor for each 2.67 sq ft of wall area, spaced no more than 32 in. horizontally and no more than 24 in. vertically under the IRC (25 in. under the IBC).

How many wall ties per square foot?

For brick veneer over wood studs, BIA Technical Note 28 says at least one anchor for each 2.67 sq ft of wall area. In high wind areas under the IRC it says one anchor for each 2 sq ft, and for buildings in Seismic Design Category D or above (and townhouses in SDC C) it says to reduce the wall area per anchor by 25 percent. Masonry backed cavity walls and engineered walls follow their own requirements.

Do I need extra wall ties around windows and doors?

Yes. BIA Technical Note 28 says to add anchors within 12 in. of openings larger than 16 in., at a maximum spacing of 3 ft on center. BIA Technical Note 44B separately recommends additional ties within 8 in. of openings and discontinuities such as windows, shelf angles and vertical expansion joints. Build what the drawings show.

How deep should wall ties be embedded in the mortar?

CMHA TEK 12-01B says the masonry specification requires connectors to be embedded at least 1 1/2 in. (38 mm) into a mortar bed of solid units, and that unit ties in hollow masonry must extend completely across the hollow units.

Can I use corrugated wall ties?

Only where they are allowed for the wall type. BIA Technical Note 44B says corrugated ties are typically used in low rise residential veneer over wood frame and are not recommended for veneer over steel studs, masonry backed cavity walls, multi wythe walls or grouted walls. Technical Note 28 says corrugated anchors should be at least No. 22 U.S. gage, with a maximum 1 in. air space where they are used, nailed to the stud within 1/2 in. of the 90 degree bend.

Should a wall tie have a drip bend in the middle?

No. BIA Technical Note 44B recommends not specifying ties with formed drips, because testing showed drips can reduce the ultimate buckling load by approximately 50 percent.

What metal should wall ties be?

Whatever the specification calls for, matched to the exposure. CMHA TEK 12-01B says carbon steel ties need galvanizing or epoxy coating, with hot dip galvanizing to ASTM A153 at 1.5 oz zinc per sq ft for wire ties in exterior walls, and that where stainless steel is specified it must be AISI Type 304 or 316.

Can I fix a bulging brick wall by adding more ties?

Not as a routine job. Have the wall assessed first. BIA Technical Note 44B describes re-anchoring systems (mechanical expansion, helical screw and epoxy adhesive types) used to add or replace ties in existing walls, and says consultation with the tie system manufacturer is essential for proper application, installation and inspection.
