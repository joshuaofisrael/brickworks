How to clean new brickwork and mortar smears

Short answer

Clean new brickwork once the mortar has hardened, typically between 24 hours and seven days after laying, using the gentlest method that works. Saturate the wall with water first, test on a 20 sq ft (2 m²) patch, apply cleaner at no more than 50 psi, and rinse at under 400 psi. Avoid unbuffered muriatic acid and abrasive blasting on new work.

Prevention beats cleaning

BIA Technical Note 20 starts on site: keep brick off the ground and covered, protect the base of walls from splashed mud, lay with techniques that limit smearing, brush excess mortar after tooling, and knock off large mortar tags with wooden paddles or non metal tools. Cover unfinished walls each night with sheeting that runs at least 2 ft down each side.

Step by step

  1. Check the brick maker's instructions. Their recommended method and cleaner come first.
  2. Do a trial patch. About 20 sq ft (2 m²), allowed to dry before you judge it. The accepted patch becomes the standard for the rest of the wall.
  3. Protect neighbours. Windows, metal, stone trim and plants can be harmed by cleaners.
  4. Saturate the wall. Soak the area and the wall below it with water so the brick does not absorb cleaner or dissolved mortar. Keep it wet until the final rinse. If spraying to pre wet, use very low pressure (under 100 psi).
  5. Apply cleaner by brush or a pump sprayer with a wide fan tip at 30 to 50 psi. Higher pressure drives solution deep into the brick, where it can cause later stains. Never let cleaner dry on the wall.
  6. Scrub with a stiff fibre brush for bucket and brush cleaning.
  7. Rinse thoroughly with low pressure water (100 to 400 psi) until all solution is gone.

BIA recommends a stainless steel fan tip of 25 to 50 degrees held at least 12 in (305 mm) from the brick, and no more than 400 psi at the nozzle unless the brick manufacturer permits more. Sand finished brick should be cleaned by bucket and brush only.

What goes wrong

  • Cleaning dry brick. The commonest failure. Dry masonry soaks up the cleaner, which can leave white scum, efflorescence, or green and brown vanadium or manganese stains.
  • Muriatic acid. BIA says not to use unbuffered muriatic (hydrochloric) or hydrofluoric acid on any brick category in its quick guide.
  • Blasting and high pressure. These remove stains by abrasion and can etch joints and strip the fired face, permanently.
  • Freezing weather. Do not clean when it is freezing or expected to freeze.

White powder that appears weeks later is likely efflorescence, covered in our efflorescence guide.

Cleaning new brickwork FAQ

How soon can I clean new brickwork?

Once the mortar has hardened, typically between 24 hours and seven days depending on the method. Do not leave it too long, because hardened smears become harder to remove.

Can I pressure wash brick?

Yes, carefully. BIA suggests under 100 psi to pre wet and 100 to 400 psi to rinse, with a 25 to 50 degree fan tip at least 12 in from the wall. Sand finished brick should be hand cleaned.

Is muriatic acid safe for cleaning brick?

BIA advises against unbuffered muriatic (hydrochloric) acid. Use the brick maker's recommended cleaner.

Why did my brick turn white after cleaning?

Usually because the wall was not saturated before the cleaner went on, so the brick absorbed the solution. It can also be efflorescence or scum from cleaner residue.
