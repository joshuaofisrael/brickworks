Weep holes in brick walls: what they do and how to place them

Short answer

Weep holes let water that gets behind brick drain out at the flashing. BIA Technical Note 7B recommends open head joint weeps (a head joint left without mortar) placed in the course directly above the flashing, spaced no more than 24 in (610 mm) apart. Do not fill them with mortar, caulk or paint.

Why brick walls need weeps

Brick veneer and cavity walls are drainage walls. Some rain always passes through the brick and mortar. It runs down the back of the brick to flashing at the base of the wall and above openings, and the weeps let it out. Without working weeps the water sits on the flashing and can find its way inward or keep the wall wet.

Weep types compared

TypeBIA guidance
Open head jointRecommended. At least 2 in (51 mm) high, spaced no more than 24 in apart. Non corrosive metal, mesh or plastic screens may be fitted to keep insects out.
Wick (rope) weepsIf used, space no more than 16 in apart; wicks should be flush with or extend slightly past the face.
Tube weepsCodes may permit openings as small as 3/16 in diameter, but BIA discourages tubes because they drain less and clog more easily.

BIA also advises against putting weeps one or more courses above the flashing, because water must build up before it can drain.

Installation tips

  • Run the flashing out to or past the face of the brick; BIA says do not stop it behind the face. A drip edge is recommended.
  • Place weeps in the joints immediately above the flashing.
  • Keep the air space clear of mortar droppings so the weeps can drain.
  • Seal any punctures in the flashing with a compatible sealant or membrane.

Homeowner checks

Look along the bottom course of a brick veneer house and above windows and doors. You should see regular open joints or vents. If they have been mortared, caulked or painted shut, or buried by raised soil or mulch, they cannot drain. Persistent white staining near the base can be a sign of water movement, see efflorescence. For tooling and joint filling, see mortar joint profiles.

Weep holes in brick walls FAQ

How far apart should weep holes be?

For open head joint weeps BIA recommends no more than 24 in (610 mm) apart. Wick weeps, if used, no more than 16 in apart.

Should weep holes be filled?

No. Weeps must stay open to drain water from behind the brick. Screens or vents that keep insects out while letting water drain are fine.

Where do weep holes go?

In the mortar joints directly above flashing: at the base of the wall and above openings such as windows and doors, and anywhere else flashing is installed.

Are plastic tube weeps OK?

Codes may allow them, but BIA discourages tube weeps because the small openings drain less and clog more easily than open head joints.
