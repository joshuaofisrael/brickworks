By the Mortar Smash editorial team. Published 9 November 2026. Sources re-read 20 November 2026.

Short answer

Leave open head joints on the flashing, no more than 24 in (610 mm) apart, and do not fill them. That spacing is the recommendation in BIA Technical Note 7B (November 2017). The same note says most building codes allow up to 33 in (838 mm). Twenty-four inches is the tighter drainage practice, not a claim that every code uses it. Follow the code and the drawings for the wall in front of you.

Why brick walls need weeps

Brick veneer and cavity walls are drainage walls. Some rain always passes through the brick and mortar. It runs down the back of the brick to flashing at the base of the wall and above openings, and the weeps let it out. The cavity those weeps serve is also where the wall ties sit, and the base of the wall is a different water problem, covered in brickwork below the DPC. Without working weeps the water sits on the flashing and can find its way inward or keep the wall wet.

Weep types compared

TypeBIA guidance
Open head jointRecommended. At least 2 in (51 mm) high, spaced no more than 24 in apart. Non corrosive metal, mesh or plastic screens may be fitted to keep insects out.
Wick (rope) weepsIf used, space no more than 16 in (406 mm) apart. The rope should be at least 16 in long, set along the back of the brick into the bottom of the air space, and finish flush with the face or 1/2 in (12.7 mm) past it.
Tube weepsCodes may permit openings as small as 3/16 in diameter, but BIA discourages tubes because they drain less and clog more easily.

BIA also advises against putting weeps one or more courses above the flashing, because water must build up before it can drain.

24 inches on a 20 foot course

A 20 ft base course is 240 in. Open head joints at both ends, with no gap larger than 24 in, fall on the even numbers: 0, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, 144, 168, 192, 216 and 240. That is 10 gaps and 11 weeps. The same 240 in at the 33 in maximum the note attributes to most codes needs 8 gaps, because 240 divided by 33 is 7.3, so 9 weeps if both ends are open. Even spacing in that case is 30 in.

Those two counts are arithmetic from the two maximums in Technical Note 7B. They are not a layout. A window head, a corner, a change in flashing height or an opening in the course starts the row again. Expansion joints break the wall into separate lengths, and each length needs its own weeps. This page is not a design for a wall. Use the spacing in the code that governs the job.

Installation tips

  • Do not stop the flashing behind the face of the brick. Technical Note 7B says flexible flashing should project from the face. A drip is recommended, though that flashing may be cut flush. Sheet metal flashing is intended to project past the face and form a drip.
  • Place weeps in the joints immediately above the flashing.
  • Keep the air space clear of mortar droppings so the weeps can drain.
  • Seal any punctures in the flashing with a compatible sealant or membrane.

Homeowner checks

Look along the bottom course of a brick veneer house and above windows and doors. You should see regular open joints or vents. If they have been mortared, caulked or painted shut, or buried by raised soil or mulch, they cannot drain. Persistent white staining near the base can be a sign of water movement, see efflorescence. For tooling and joint filling, see mortar joint profiles.

Weep holes in brick walls FAQ

How far apart should weep holes be?

For open head joint weeps BIA recommends no more than 24 in (610 mm) apart. Wick weeps, if used, no more than 16 in apart.

Should weep holes be filled?

No. Weeps must stay open to drain water from behind the brick. Screens or vents that keep insects out while letting water drain are fine.

Where do weep holes go?

In the mortar joints directly above flashing: at the base of the wall and above openings such as windows and doors, and anywhere else flashing is installed.

Are plastic tube weeps OK?

Codes may allow openings as small as 3/16 in, but BIA Technical Note 7B discourages tube weeps because the small openings drain less and clog more easily than open head joints.

Do building codes require weep holes every 24 inches?

Not according to BIA Technical Note 7B (November 2017). The note recommends open head joint weeps no more than 24 in apart, and it says most building codes permit 33 in. Build to the code and the project documents for the job.
