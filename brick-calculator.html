How many bricks for common jobs?

Short answer: a 5 m long, 1.2 m high freestanding garden wall one brick thick needs about 712 UK bricks, or 783 with 10% waste. A half-brick (single-leaf) wall uses about 60 UK bricks per m², so a 4 m × 2.4 m extension wall with 2 m² of windows and doors needs about 451 bricks, or 496 with waste. In the US, a modular single-leaf wall uses about 6.9 bricks per square foot — see bricks per square foot for the worked example.

How many bricks per square foot?

Short answer: about 6.9 US modular bricks per square foot for a single-leaf (veneer) wall with a ⅜ in joint, or about 13.7 per square foot for a solid wall two wythes thick. Add 5% to 10% for cuts and breakages.

US estimating tables often quote 6.75 modular bricks per sq ft. That figure comes from modular coursing (three courses rise exactly 8 inches), which implies a slightly thicker bed joint than a fixed ⅜ in. This calculator uses the brick size plus the joint you enter — for the default US modular ⅜ in joint that is about 6.85 per sq ft, rounded to 6.9 in the table below. Change the joint to match your spec if you need the modular-coursing number.

Worked example: a garden seat or patio wall 20 ft long × 3 ft high (60 sq ft), two wythes thick in US modular brick, needs about 823 bricks, or 905 with 10% waste, plus about 9.3 cu ft of wet mortar (~218 lb cement and ~970 lb sand at a 1:4 mix). How many bricks for a brick pier or pillar?

Short answer: a square 1½-brick pier (327.5 mm, the usual size for a garden-wall or gate pier) takes about 4½ standard UK bricks per course, which is roughly 60 bricks per metre of height. A 1-brick pier (215 mm) takes 2 bricks per course (about 27 per metre) and a solid 2-brick pier (440 mm) takes 8 per course (about 107 per metre). A pair of 1.8 m gate piers at 1½ bricks needs about 216 bricks, or 238 with 10% waste.

Wall area doesn't work for piers, so count by course instead: bricks per course × number of courses. UK bricks lay at 75 mm per course (65 mm brick plus a 10 mm joint); US modular brick lays three courses to 8 inches. A 12 × 12 in US modular pier works the same way as the UK 1½-brick pier (about 4½ bricks per course), and a 16 × 16 in pier takes 8. The half brick per course comes from cut bats in the bond, so some courses use 4 and some 5.

How many bricks for a brick-on-edge coping?

Short answer: a brick-on-edge coping on a one-brick (215 mm) garden wall takes about 13⅓ standard UK bricks per metre of wall, because each brick stands on its side and fills 75 mm of length (65 mm brick plus a 10 mm joint). A 5 m garden wall needs 67 coping bricks, or 74 with 10% waste, on top of the bricks for the wall itself. In US modular brick, a rowlock cap across a two-wythe (7⅝ in) wall takes about 4.6 bricks per linear foot (2¼ in brick plus a ⅜ in joint), so a 20 ft seat wall needs 92, or 102 with 10% waste.

Coping is counted by length, not area: divide the wall length by the brick height plus one joint. A double brick-on-edge course doubles the figure, and walls finished with tile creasing under the coping need the creasing tiles counted separately. Brick-on-edge only works when the brick length matches the wall thickness, so a half-brick (102.5 mm) wall is usually finished with a coping stone or a brick-on-edge course laid along the wall instead.

How the calculation works

Every brick takes up its own face plus one mortar joint on the side and one on top. So the number of bricks in one square metre of a single-leaf wall is:

bricks per m² = 1 ÷ ((brick length + joint) × (brick height + joint)), with sizes in metres.

For a UK brick with a 10 mm joint that is 1 ÷ (0.225 × 0.075) ≈ 59.3, which is why builders quote "about 60 bricks per square metre". A wall one brick thick, or a cavity wall with brick on both sides, uses two leaves, so roughly double. Openings for doors and windows are subtracted before multiplying, and a waste allowance (typically 5% to 10%) covers cuts, breakages and chipped faces.

Mortar is whatever fills the wall that isn't brick: the wall's volume minus the bricks' volume. To turn wet mortar into dry ingredients the calculator uses the common estimating rule that dry materials shrink by about 30% when mixed, a loose bulk density of about 1,440 kg/m³ for cement and 1,600 kg/m³ for damp building sand.

Common questions

How many bricks are in a 1 m² wall?

About 60 standard UK bricks for a single-leaf wall, or about 120 for a solid wall one brick thick. Add 5% to 10% for waste.

How many bricks do I need for a garden wall?

Multiply length by height to get the face area, choose "one brick thick" if it is a freestanding 215 mm wall (most garden walls over about 450 mm high are built that way for stability), and add waste. Don't forget the coping or brick-on-edge course on top, which uses extra bricks (about 13⅓ per metre; see coping).

How many bricks do I need for a 5 metre garden wall?

A freestanding garden wall 5 m long and 1.2 m high, one brick thick (215 mm) in standard UK bricks, needs about 712 bricks, or 783 with 10% waste. It also needs about 0.3 m³ of wet mortar, roughly five 25 kg bags of cement and 500 kg of sand at a 1:4 mix. Add extra bricks for the coping course and any piers.

How many bricks do I need for a brick pillar?

A square 1½-brick pier (327.5 mm) in standard UK bricks takes about 4½ bricks per course, or roughly 60 bricks per metre of height, so a 1.8 m gate pier needs about 108 bricks and a pair about 216 (238 with 10% waste). A 1-brick pier takes about 27 bricks per metre and a solid 2-brick pier about 107. In US modular brick a 12 × 12 in pier also takes about 4½ bricks per course and a 16 × 16 in pier 8. See bricks for piers and pillars.

How many bricks do I need for a brick-on-edge coping?

About 13⅓ standard UK bricks per metre of wall for a brick-on-edge coping on a one-brick (215 mm) wall, so a 5 m garden wall needs 67, or 74 with 10% waste. A double brick-on-edge course needs twice as many. In US modular brick a rowlock cap on a two-wythe wall takes about 4.6 bricks per linear foot, so a 20 ft wall needs 92, or 102 with 10% waste. See bricks for coping.

Does the bond pattern change the number of bricks?

Not for the same wall thickness. Stretcher, English and Flemish bonds all fill the same area; they just arrange the headers and stretchers differently. What changes the count is the thickness. See our guide to brick bonds.

How much mortar do I need per 1,000 bricks?

For UK bricks in a single-leaf wall, roughly 0.3 m³ of wet mortar per 1,000 solid bricks before waste, and more for frogged bricks. The calculator works it out from your exact size and joint, and the mortar calculator works it out from a brick count, with cement and sand per 1,000 bricks.

How many bricks per square foot for a modular brick wall?

About 6.9 US modular bricks (7⅝ × 3⅝ × 2¼ in) per square foot of single-leaf wall with a ⅜ in mortar joint, or about 13.7 for a solid wall two wythes thick. Estimating tables often quote 6.75 per sq ft because modular coursing assumes three courses rise exactly 8 inches, which uses a slightly thicker bed joint than ⅜ in. Add 5% to 10% for waste.

How many bricks for a 20 ft by 3 ft seat wall?

A freestanding garden seat or patio wall 20 ft long and 3 ft high, two wythes thick in US modular brick, needs about 823 bricks, or 905 with 10% waste. It also needs about 9.3 cu ft of wet mortar, roughly 218 lb of cement and 970 lb of sand at a 1:4 mix. Add extra bricks for a coping course and any piers.
