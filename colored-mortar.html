Colored mortar: getting even color in brick joints

Plain-English mortar guidance for bricklayers and site work

Short answer

Use pigments that meet ASTM C979 and as little as possible. BIA Technical Note 8 limits metallic oxide pigments to 10 percent of the cement weight and carbon black to 2 percent, halved for mortar cement and masonry cement. Even colour comes from accurate batching, the same sand, the same water, no retempering, and tooling every area at the same stiffness.

Pigment rules

  • Use mineral oxide pigments complying with ASTM C979.
  • Metallic oxide pigment: no more than 10 percent of cement content by weight in cement-lime mortar.
  • Carbon black: no more than 2 percent of cement content by weight.
  • For mortar cement and masonry cement, halve those limits.
  • Avoid pigments containing Prussian blue, cadmium lithopone, or zinc and lead chromates.
  • Premix cement and colour in large, controlled quantities, or buy pre coloured mortar.

BIA warns that excess pigment may seriously impair the strength and durability of the mortar, and that paint pigments are not suitable because they do not meet ASTM C979.

What changes the shade on site

FactorEffect
Mixing waterBIA notes colour uniformity varies with the amount of mixing water; batch water consistently
RetemperingDilutes colour; BIA recommends not retempering coloured mortar
Brick moistureWet or dry brick changes how fast the joint dries and its shade
Tooling timeEarly tooling dries lighter, late tooling darker (BIA Technical Note 7B)
SandThe sand is the largest ingredient and drives colour and texture; keep one source
CleaningHarsh acid cleaning can etch and lighten joints

Batching for even colour

  1. Measure by consistent volumes or weight, not shovels of "about right".
  2. Mix pigment with the dry ingredients long enough to disperse it fully.
  3. Mix smaller batches that are used without retempering.
  4. Build a sample panel and let it cure before approving the colour; wet mortar looks darker than cured mortar.

For old buildings the NPS repointing brief favours matching colour mainly through the sand, using white portland cement where cement is used, rather than strong pigments. See how to repoint brick.

Colored mortar FAQ

How much pigment can you add to mortar?

Metallic oxide pigments up to 10 percent of the cement weight and carbon black up to 2 percent, halved for mortar cement and masonry cement, per BIA Technical Note 8.

Why is my coloured mortar patchy?

Usually inconsistent water, retempering, different sand, or tooling different areas at different stiffness. Wet brick versus dry brick also changes the shade.

Does mortar dry lighter or darker?

Mortar usually dries lighter than it looks wet. Judge colour on a cured sample panel.

Can I retemper coloured mortar?

BIA recommends not retempering coloured mortar because extra water dilutes the colour. Mix smaller batches instead.
