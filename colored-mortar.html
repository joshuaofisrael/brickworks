Colored mortar: 10 percent pigment cap, 5 percent on masonry cement

Short answer

Use as little ASTM C979 pigment as will do the job, and prefer a colored sand if the sand can carry the color. BIA Technical Note 8 (March 2020) caps most metallic oxide pigments at 10 percent of the cement content by weight in cement-lime mortar, and at 5 percent in masonry-cement or mortar-cement mortar. Carbon black is 2 percent and 1 percent on that same split. Even color still depends on one sand source, one water measure, no retempering, and tooling every area at the same stiffness.

Pigment rules

  • Use mineral oxide pigments complying with ASTM C979.
  • Metallic oxide pigment: no more than 10 percent of cement content by weight in cement-lime mortar.
  • Carbon black: no more than 2 percent of cement content by weight.
  • For mortar cement and masonry cement, halve those limits.
  • Avoid pigments containing Prussian blue, cadmium lithopone, or zinc and lead chromates.
  • Premix cement and colour in large, controlled quantities, or buy pre coloured mortar.

BIA warns that excess pigment may seriously impair the strength and durability of the mortar, and that paint pigments are not suitable because they do not meet ASTM C979. The same note prefers colored aggregates (the sand, or ground granite, marble or stone) when they can produce the color, because that color is more permanent and does not weaken the mortar the way an overload of pigment can. Sand choice is in building sand vs sharp sand.

What 10 percent is on a real sack

The cap is a ceiling, not a target, and it is a share of the cement, not of the whole mix. The QUIKRETE portland cement data sheet (revised August 2022) lists a 94 lb (42.6 kg) sack. Ten percent of that sack is 9.4 lb of metallic oxide pigment. Two percent, the carbon-black ceiling for cement-lime mortar, is 1.88 lb. That arithmetic uses the BIA percentages and the sack weight printed on the sheet. It is not a dose from a pigment maker.

Masonry cement is the other case. The QUIKRETE Type S masonry cement data sheet lists a 70 lb (31.8 kg) bag, and Technical Note 8 halves the ceilings for masonry cement and mortar cement. Five percent of 70 lb is 3.5 lb of metallic oxide. One percent is 0.7 lb of carbon black. Read the bag in front of you. A "general purpose" masonry cement is not portland cement, so the 10 percent figure does not transfer. Which cement you are coloring is the Type N, S, M or O decision, and the gauging is in how to mix mortar.

If two pigments are combined, Technical Note 8 says the total of all pigments must not exceed any one of those individual ceilings. Do not add extra pigment to a premixed colored mortar unless that manufacturer says to. Premixed bags are covered in preblended vs site-mixed mortar.

Batching for even colour

  1. Measure by consistent volumes or weight, not shovels of "about right".
  2. Mix pigment with the dry ingredients long enough to disperse it fully.
  3. Mix smaller batches that are used without retempering.
  4. Build a sample panel and let it cure before approving the colour; wet mortar looks darker than cured mortar.

On historic masonry, National Park Service Preservation Brief 2 (October 1998) says to match the unweathered interior of the old mortar. If natural sand or a colored aggregate such as crushed marble or brick dust cannot get there, a modern pigment may be necessary. The brief also says white, non-staining portland cement can match some old mortars better than grey cement, and that it should not be assumed for every old wall, because the original mortar may have been mixed with grey cement. Its pigment ceilings match the cement-lime numbers above: no more than 10 percent of the portland cement by weight, and carbon black no more than 2 percent. The repointing sequence is in how to repoint brick. Retempering, which the brief also tells you not to do once portland cement is in a lime putty mix, is timed in how long mortar stays usable.

Colored mortar FAQ

How much pigment can you add to mortar?

Metallic oxide pigments up to 10 percent of the cement weight and carbon black up to 2 percent, halved for mortar cement and masonry cement, per BIA Technical Note 8.

Why is my coloured mortar patchy?

Usually inconsistent water, retempering, different sand, or tooling different areas at different stiffness. Wet brick versus dry brick also changes the shade.

Does mortar dry lighter or darker?

Mortar usually dries lighter than it looks wet. Judge colour on a cured sample panel.

Can I retemper coloured mortar?

BIA Technical Note 8 (March 2020) says not to retemper colored mortar, because extra water dilutes the color. Mix smaller batches instead.

Is the pigment limit the same for masonry cement?

No. Technical Note 8 sets 10 percent of the cement content by weight for most metallic oxide pigments in cement-lime mortar, and 5 percent for masonry cement or mortar cement. Carbon black is 2 percent and 1 percent on the same split.

Check another joint decision

Color is one thing that makes two joints look unlike. The profile, the thickness and the time the mortar is still usable change the wall as well.

  • Mortar joint profiles: concave and V shed water; the tooling time also shifts the shade.
  • Mortar joint thickness: 3/8 in in US modular work, 10 mm in much UK work.
